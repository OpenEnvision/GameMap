# Studying Levels and Transferring Their Relationships

Sources: [How to study a level](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/overview), [Single player studies](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/sp), [Undead Burg](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/sp/undead-burg), [Assassins](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/sp/assassins), [Multiplayer studies](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/mp), [Chill Out](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/mp/chill-out), [Real world studies](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/irl), [Disneyland](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/studies/irl/disneyland). Adapted and reorganized under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. These are interpretations of the book's analyses, not new recorded playtests.

## 1. Study a level as a playable work

Do not begin and end with a top-down screenshot.

Establish:
- Game/version, mode, rules, player state, and relevant mechanics.
- What evidence is available: direct play, recording, map, editor data, or an author's analysis.
- What question motivates the study.
- Which observations are yours and which come from the source.

Useful study passes:
1. Play or observe without a thesis to notice surprises.
2. Reconstruct routes, heights, gates, resources, encounters, and states.
3. Repeat with attention to one relationship.
4. Form an interpretation and look for counterexamples.
5. Identify the assumptions a transfer would require.

When direct play is unavailable, say so. A close reading can still be useful without pretending it is an empirical user study.

## 2. Undead Burg — Dark Souls

**Source status:** substantial but explicitly unfinished.

### Central reading

The article treats a relatively linear castle area as instruction in exploration patterns. Branches, elevation, danger, loot, repeated spaces, and shortcuts teach players how to read the game.

Useful relationships:
- Visible or implied destinations encourage investigation.
- Optional detours test curiosity and expose information or rewards.
- Encounters interact with narrow circulation, ranged pressure, and approach direction.
- Returning to recognizable places turns an initially confusing network into learned geography.
- The bonfire and later connections change the cost and meaning of repeating a route.

### Transferable question

Can a short area teach players how this game hides and reconnects space, without making them memorize arbitrary instructions?

### Prototype adaptation

Build one required route, a readable but optional detour, and a return connection. Place threats so that observation and approach matter. Test whether an unfamiliar player:
- Notices the possibility of exploration.
- Distinguishes progress from a detour.
- Recognizes the return connection.
- Uses learned knowledge on a retry.

This is an adaptation, not the article's complete diagram or a recipe for copying the level.

### Do not transfer blindly

Dark Souls' camera, combat commitment, death, resource recovery, and repeated traversal support these relationships. A game with instant unrestricted movement, no retry cost, or different combat readability may need another structure.

## 3. Assassins — Thief: The Dark Project

**Source status:** substantive close reading.

### Central reading

The mission changes the player's relationship to the City over time: an initially familiar public environment becomes a place for following targets, entering private territory, and later navigating danger.

The article connects:
- An initial event that gives the player an investigative objective.
- Following assassins through the City.
- Discovering and infiltrating Ramirez's private space.
- Looting and returning through an environment whose meaning and threats have changed.

Spatial form, access, patrols, visibility, and sequence support both stealth and an interpretation of power: private authority increasingly occupies public space.

### Transferable question

Can reusing a place under changed conditions produce a new activity and a new interpretation, rather than simply save production work?

### Prototype adaptation

Create a route used first for observation and later for escape or cautious return. Change selected access conditions, actors, or knowledge, not necessarily the entire geometry.

Test:
- Can players understand what changed?
- Does prior spatial knowledge remain useful?
- Are new threats readable rather than arbitrary?
- Does the return support the intended meaning, not merely added difficulty?

### Do not transfer blindly

Light/shadow stealth, guard behavior, movement, and information matter. A conventional combat game cannot reproduce the mission's relationships by copying a mansion floor plan.

## 4. Chill Out — Halo: Combat Evolved

**Source status:** substantial multiplayer analysis by Andrew Yoder.

### Central reading

Chill Out is a compact asymmetric competitive arena. The article begins with Halo's rules because geometry cannot be evaluated independently of movement, shields, health, weapons, and pickup behavior.

Useful relationships:
- Rooms, corridors, elevation, and shortcuts produce a dense circulation network.
- Weapon and item locations create reasons to move and contest.
- Important positions provide advantages with costs and vulnerabilities.
- Visual distinctions and repeated traversal help players learn the arena.
- Resource timing and control change what a route or room means during a match.

### Transferable question

Does each strong position participate in a wider exchange of information, resources, exposure, and mobility?

### Prototype adaptation

Use a small asymmetric network with a few distinct spaces. Give a valuable position a specific advantage, then test its access, resources, view, vulnerability, and escape.

Measure relevant contact and rotation times; observe whether control can change hands. Do not claim balance from symmetry or a clean floor plan.

### Do not transfer blindly

A weapon's effectiveness, shield recovery, player speed, and item rules can reverse a positional advantage. Keep source-specific numerical claims tied to the exact game/version rather than treating them as generic shooter metrics.

## 5. Disneyland — California

**Source status:** substantive real-world study.

### Central reading

The article relates themed physical space to game-like spatial organization while also addressing cultural and political meanings.

It discusses:
- Designers' site study and field-trip practices.
- Layering and revealing space.
- **Weenies:** conspicuous distant attractions or landmarks that draw visitors onward.
- **Berms:** boundaries that organize and screen the environment.
- **Dark rides:** carefully staged, sequential experiences.
- The conflict between presentation, control, history, and cultural representation.

### Transferable question

How do landmarks, concealed transitions, boundaries, and staged sequences organize expectation—and whose view of the world do they normalize?

### Prototype adaptation

Build an approach where a destination is visible before its route is fully revealed. Vary enclosure and reveal while maintaining orientation. Test whether curiosity and comprehension coexist.

### Do not transfer blindly

Visitors' bodies, fatigue, queues, weather, accessibility, money, and social conditions differ from game avatars. Do not turn an analysis of a controlled commercial environment into an uncritical ideal for all public space or all games.

## 7. Transfer matrix

Use this adaptation when borrowing a pattern:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Observed relationship | What connects actions, space, information, and consequence? |
| Source evidence | Direct play, author analysis, recording, or map |
| Enabling rules | Camera, movement, combat, persistence, resources, audience |
| Intended effect | What the relationship seems to accomplish |
| Alternative explanation | What else could cause that effect |
| Target differences | Which enabling conditions are absent or changed |
| Small prototype | Minimum reconstruction of the relationship |
| Test | Observable behavior that would support or challenge the transfer |

A successful transfer preserves a useful relationship under new conditions. It does not require copying the source's theme, silhouette, room count, or reputation.
