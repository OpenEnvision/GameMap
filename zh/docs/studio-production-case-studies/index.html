工作室制作案例与验收经验

英文原文

Study how game teams handle procedural generation, asset budgets, build feedback, and gameplay systems. Each case links the original engineering account and turns its lessons into a workflow template for your own project.

Main conclusions

  1. Catalog asset readiness as multiple states: authored, converted, dependency-complete, loaded, initialized, activated, saved and reopened.
  2. Separate preview shortcuts from release acceptance. A useful cached or stale preview is not a fully current build.
  3. Keep hardware, driver, cache state, internal resolution and exact engine fork in performance evidence.
  4. Treat behavior coverage, regression coverage and artistic quality as different acceptance dimensions.
  5. Capture negative results and unsupported rollback paths, not just successful API invocations.

Find a production lesson

Fortnite Nanite asset conditioning

Source: Fortnite Nanite asset conditioning Context: 2023-01-26; UE5.1 Fortnite Chapter 4

Published evidence. Epic found opaque geometric foliage faster than masked cards for this content. Wind was baked from Houdini Vellum into textures indexed by branch UV data. Preserve Area addressed disappearing distant canopy; it was intended only for affected foliage. World Position Offset bounds could cause disappearing geometry or excessive culling conservatism.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Build a foliage conditioning comparison with fixed camera paths, near/far canopy coverage, animated bounds, base-pass and shadow timings. Compare cards and geometry on target hardware. Record source mesh, UV encoding and wind bake revision. Treat the historical optimization as a hypothesis, not a universal rule.

Fortnite shadow budgeting and failure cases

Source: Fortnite shadow budgeting and failure cases Context: 2023-01-26; UE5.1 Fortnite Chapter 4

Published evidence. Moving sun and animated foliage undermined shadow caching. Throttled invalidation introduced page artifacts; near-camera trees were expensive. Epic budgeted uncached sun shadows and used a diagnostic console variable to identify non-Nanite page-area offenders. Shadow proxy benefits were mainly close-range, not a blanket distant-LOD saving.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Use traversal scenarios that look upward inside dense trees, change time of day and cross indoor/outdoor lighting. Capture page invalidation, GPU timings and flicker artifacts. Compare cold and stable views. Keep any resolution or cache-policy change reversible and evaluate art direction with the performance delta.

Fortnite lighting budget and gameplay readability

Source: Fortnite lighting budget and gameplay readability Context: 2023-01-26; UE5.1 with explicitly identified later fix

Published evidence. For its console 60-fps target, Fortnite allocated about four milliseconds to GI and reflections and selected software tracing. Foliage occlusion, water streaking, exposure swings and dark interiors required fixes or art controls. One last-minute water fix was explicitly absent from the public UE5.1 release.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Record internal resolution separately from display resolution. Evaluate a lighting change across moving-camera water, dense foliage and interior transitions; measure readability and temporal stability alongside GPU cost. Tag engine fork/revision so a shipped-game result does not imply the same feature exists in a nominal public release.

PSO precaching production limits

Source: PSO precaching production limits Context: 2025-02-04; historical UE5.2 introduction and UE5.5 limitations

Published evidence. Bundled caches depend on discovered content permutations and can miss dynamic material changes. Load-time precaching narrows the combinations, but driver caches make later runs materially different. The article identifies global graphics shaders as a UE5.5 gap and mobile precache timeouts as another first-use hitch risk.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Create separately labeled cold-driver-cache and warm-cache performance runs. Exercise skins, material swaps, quality presets and newly streamed objects. Record GPU, driver, RHI, shader content revision, cache state and loading completion. Do not erase user caches as part of an unapproved diagnostic; use an isolated test profile.

Editor revision integrity and team iteration

Source: Editor revision integrity and team iteration Context: historical Epic whitepaper; exact publication date not established

Published evidence. Epic describes mismatched binary modules producing crashes and memory corruption, and old editors discarding unknown serialized properties. Its solution stamps editor build changelists into assets and rejects newer-created assets in older editors. UGS exposes build results and human feedback, replacing a slow promotion bottleneck.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Before an agent opens or resaves a project, compare editor build identity, modules, plugin revisions and asset provenance. Keep a disposable workspace and write a manifest. Run reopen checks with the intended consuming build. A green compile badge must remain separate from an asset integrity or gameplay acceptance result.

Destiny 2 asset pipeline scalability

Source: Destiny 2 asset pipeline scalability Context: Brandon Moro, Bungie; GDC2018 slides

Published evidence. Bungie replaced a highly connected task graph with smaller per-asset graphs, explicit dependencies, asset-level caching and load-time references. Reports explain cache reuse and invalidation causes. Local iteration could deliberately accept stale ambient occlusion or missing audio, demonstrating that preview freshness differs from complete production output.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Model preview and release-ready builds as different contracts. Store dependency closure, input hashes, cache hit/miss reasons and stale exceptions per asset. Profile graph traversal and packaging separately from transform execution. Reject preview-only relaxed dependencies in release validation; never label cache reuse as a newly executed transform.

Horizon asset activation and streaming

Source: Horizon asset activation and streaming Context: studio-linked talk; listing dated 2023-03-21; Horizon Zero Dawn system

Published evidence. Decima separates deserialization, link resolution, initialization and world activation. Content graphs use streaming links to partition dependencies; initialization respects dependency order. Files are reference-counted and shared objects are loaded once. The system moved beyond corridor loading and avoided packing during iteration.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Expose loaded, initialized and activated as distinct readiness states. Stress streaming with fast travel, reversals and overlapping dependencies; monitor resident bytes and delayed activation. Check that unloading one consumer does not invalidate shared content still used elsewhere. This is a proposed cross-engine test, not a public Decima API binding.

Horizon quest graph successes and rollback limit

Source: Horizon quest graph successes and rollback limit Context: Leszek Szczepanski, Guerrilla; GDC2017 slides

Published evidence. Quest steps form a cause-and-effect graph with a finite verb vocabulary. Completion opens or closes graph regions, enabling nonlinear paths and repeated-pattern generation. The talk identifies a failure: the story metaphor did not support reversing previously completed events; its verb vocabulary also needed expansion.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Generate quest tests from typed verbs and reachability, then cover save/reload, abandonment, failure, repeated events and rollback. Keep narrative quality assessment separate from state-machine validity. Report unsupported verbs and irreversible transitions before authoring. This recipe needs a project-specific quest-state adapter.

Learned Motion Matching integration limits

Source: Learned Motion Matching integration limits Context: Ubisoft La Forge; 2020-07-30 research explanation

Published evidence. Motion matching selects from supplied motion, so separate walking and drinking examples do not create walking-while-drinking. Feature and motion databases consume increasing memory. Learned Motion Matching targets replacement of lookup machinery; transition blending, foot locking and IK remain relevant integration steps.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Build a behavior-coverage matrix before choosing or generating motion. Measure trajectory error, foot sliding, hand contacts, transition discontinuity, memory and runtime cost. Include held-out behavior combinations and mark absent training coverage explicitly. A research demonstration does not prove an accessible production SDK or successful engine integration.

League automated testing reliability

Source: League automated testing reliability Context: Jim Merrill, Riot; 2016-02-26 historical BVS system

Published evidence. Riot separated executor, game driver and test scripts, using RPC commands and state queries. Conditional waits replaced sleeps. Tests passed a staging period before blocking builds; deployment-blocking smoke checks were distinct from functional coverage. The reported system complemented manual testing rather than eliminating it.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Expose explicit observations and predicates instead of arbitrary delays. Separate infrastructure failure, assertion failure, timeout and flaky-test quarantine. Preserve the same scenario locally and in CI, with driver-specific setup. Avoid promoting newly generated tests to build blockers until repeated reliability evidence supports that policy.

League replay determinism engineering

Source: League replay determinism engineering Context: Riot engineering; 2017-06-20 historical server system

Published evidence. Riot targeted gameplay-essential state rather than all memory. It recorded frame inputs and variable delta times, unified clocks and gameplay PRNG use, and isolated network state. Pointer-based ordering and uninitialized values caused divergences. A seed alone was insufficient. The reported server kept gameplay updates effectively single-threaded: delayed workers blocked the main thread deterministically, and game data were preloaded to avoid I/O races.

Proposed agent-ready recipe. Define the state projection and tolerance before calling a run reproducible. Record input order, initial configuration, clock deltas, RNG state, content/build identity and asynchronous completion policy. Compare per-frame hashes and locate the first divergence. Scope claims to tested platforms and builds; visual similarity is not deterministic replay.

Dead Cells: generation must preserve the authored experience

Primary source and scope. Sébastien Benard’s firsthand The Level Design of Dead Cells displays 2020-02-15 and republishes an earlier post whose link now redirects. Treat this as a republication date, not a game-build date. His biography confirms his Motion Twin lead developer/game-designer role.

Published account. Benard reports insufficient capacity for full handcrafting, while a procedural prototype refreshed combat but produced incoherent levels. The hybrid retained fixed world progression, biome-specific handmade rooms and authored graphs specifying paths and special rooms. Candidate rooms had to satisfy graph interfaces and purpose; monster placement added quantity and compatibility constraints. A 2022-09-06 author reply says the brute-force approach changed because of Switch-port difficulties. It does not specify the revised implementation or release version; do not label it verified Wave Function Collapse.

Workflow template. Separate visual variety from preservation of design intent. Before assembling rooms, record required progression, room purpose, traversal affordances and encounter constraints. A useful proposed acceptance packet would contain the graph, chosen room IDs, collision/traversal checks and a playable trace with representative views. Evaluate the intended rhythm and biome identity independently of graph validity: a connected map can still be badly paced. Add a contradiction/unsatisfied-room outcome rather than concealing repeated random attempts behind a successful final image.

Limits. This is retrospective developer evidence, not a controlled comparison or verified current implementation. An available integration and any AI-creator benefit would need independent evidence.

Far Cry 5: stale previews and excessive automation can invalidate feedback

Primary source and scope. Étienne Carrier, Ubisoft, Procedural World Generation of Far Cry 5, GDC 2018, with 145-page primary slide PDF. The event listing establishes GDC 2018, not an exact talk day. Findings concern the historical Far Cry 5 pipeline, not a public Houdini/Dunia release. The SideFX-hosted presentation page is dated 2018-06-05.

Published account, PDF pages 139–143, with nightly rebuilding described on pages 10–11. Gradient biome painting was supported technically but overlapping colors were difficult to debug, and acceptable results did not require gradients. Separately cached terrain data saved some processing but required artists to rebake after terraforming. Missed refreshes made local results differ from the correctly rebuilt nightly world; the team moved terrain-data generation inside consuming tools. Carrier also reports that the art direction’s dense forests obstructed AI and large-animal navigation. The production compromise bounded density by gameplay needs. Artists preferred manually shaping riverbeds despite an available automatic carving feature.

Supporting design boundary. Ubisoft’s 2018-03-26 interview with associate level-design directors Alain Greco and Andrejs Verlis distinguishes procedural placement of natural assets from the largely handcrafted map. Navigation, player progression and landscape-led wayfinding remained deliberate design concerns.

Workflow template. Feedback should identify the dependency generation that produced the viewed scene. A proposed regression fixture changes terrain after caching, compares a local preview with a clean rebuild, and verifies that downstream masks, placement and navigation use the intended revision. Preserve before/after views alongside input hashes and rebuild status; do not interpret a visually appealing stale preview as evidence about the final build. Pair density/style judgments with agent-size-specific navigation and route-readability checks. Review artist overrides as desired controls, not automatically as defects to eliminate. Any automation tradeoff should include user-visible complexity and reproducibility, not only transform runtime.

Limits. These are explicit production anecdotes without controlled effect sizes. The reviewed materials do not supply the internal tools, a reproducible benchmark or current engine compatibility. Their lessons motivate proposed checks; they do not demonstrate visual-agent or harness improvements.

EA AutoPlayers: learned locomotion inside controlled test scenarios

Primary source and scope. EA/SEED's CoG 2023 publication page identifies a production-integration experiment in Battlefield 2042 and Dead Space (2023). The paper, arXiv v1 dated 2023-07-19, describes historical internal systems, not a downloadable GameMap adapter. Checked 2026-10-19.

Published engineering observations, §§II–V, VII. Existing scripted AutoPlayers retained test objectives while learned controllers handled helicopter and zero-gravity locomotion. Exported ONNX models were packaged with game data; deployed inference reused training's observation-collection code. Structured state avoided per-agent rendering cost and sensitivity to graphical changes. The authors distinguish prototype, stripped test-range and production environments, explicitly warning that removing visual content changes image-based observations. Accelerating simulation caused parked helicopters to fall through the ground in their experiment. They recommend a stable game revision, continued testing of the training setup as production changes, and retaining scripted control where it already works. These are integration lessons, not a controlled estimate of improved bug yield, coverage or player enjoyment.

Release and transfer boundary. The inspected paper and EA publication page do not establish a public release of the in-house trainer, AutoPlayers integration, game projects or trained models. An ONNX output format is not sufficient to reproduce their observation schema, game-side controls or proprietary runtime. No current installation or asset/model entitlement was verified.

Comparison template. Compare a scripted baseline with replacement of one explicitly identified controller, keeping scenario objectives and failure oracles fixed. Record game revision, observation/action schema, model identity, timestep and test-range/production differences. Retain structured transitions alongside visual evidence when inspecting generated scenes. Before using stripped assets or faster simulation to reduce evaluation cost, verify the intended visual and collision observations separately; count invalid simulations and retraining costs rather than silently dropping them. Keep route completion, explored behavior, triaged defects and designer/player judgments as separate outcomes. This proposed test design has not been implemented or evaluated.

Marvel’s Spider-Man: preserve and revalidate authored encounters after world edits

Primary evidence and scope. David Santiago’s SIGGRAPH 2019 paper describes Insomniac’s historical PS4 production pipeline. Read §§1–3, especially the second page’s repair and diagnostic workflow.

Published account. Street/alley curves and building primitives supplied an initial procedural city; artists subsequently refined it. Encounter placement consumed ground/building geometry, road data, exclusion volumes and occupied locations, applying density, clearance and orientation constraints. An arrest example required at least 150 metres between placements and more than nine metres from crosswalks. City changes could invalidate already-polished encounters. A tagging mechanism distinguished adjusted content: reprocessing could preserve it when critical requirements still held, remove an unusable placement, or flag a repairable one. Inspection outputs included debug-camera coordinates, colored volumes and spatial annotations. Validation could adjust, flag or remove invalid assets and gameplay markup.

Workflow template. Make regeneration a reviewable change set. A proposed fixture would edit a street or rooftop beside a manually adjusted encounter, then retain the previous artifact, new input revision, preservation decision and reasons. Test clearance and route validity after the edit, and capture a camera view at every flagged location. Require project-specific approval before destructive cleanup; record unresolved placements rather than reporting the remaining valid ones as complete success.

Limits. This is an author report, not a downloadable pipeline or measured agent benchmark. The paper does not establish lossless preservation, universally valid placement distances, or autonomous artistic judgment. Its use of “AI” for placement improvements is not evidence of LLM or generative-agent involvement.

Ghost of Tsushima: biome changes must propagate into environmental effects

Primary evidence and scope. Environment Art Lead Joanna Wang’s July 2020 account and Lead VFX Artist Matt Vainio’s January 2021 breakdown describe the original game’s production. They address artistic control and the maintenance cost of a changing world.

Published account. Wang describes intentionally restricting foliage variety within biomes and using procedural tools to accommodate later type/density changes. Vainio reports that attaching falling-leaf effects to dense tree instances was too expensive; short activation distances could leave falling leaves unseen while players rode past. Growing separate emitters from masks introduced gaps, while local rule fixes could damage other forests. The replacement sampled a runtime biome map, combined with terrain/material/wind inputs, through a camera-following ambience system. Some effects retained the growth workflow. In both approaches, environmental edits could carry their effects with them rather than requiring wholesale manual replacement.

Workflow template. Treat a biome edit as a cross-system dependency change. A proposed review would move a forest boundary, regenerate or refresh affected outputs, and inspect both the edited area and untouched reference regions. Include mounted traversal and distant views, recording gaps, biome mismatch and particle cost alongside still images. Keep artistic distribution controls and explicit exceptions; a successful local repair should not silently pass as a world-wide regression check.

Limits. The articles provide first-hand failure/recovery narratives, not an open tool implementation or controlled productivity result. The VFX page now displays an additional May 2026 date; no revision history establishes which passages changed. Preserve the original-game context rather than treating it as a current-engine specification.

Turn an established art motif into a requested direction cue

In the official May 2020 Q&A, Jason Connell says wind and movement were early art-direction goals. A couple of years into production, the team tried making that established wind indicate destinations, then refined it and extended its use to optional collectible searches. Connell's judgment that the idea worked is qualitative, without a reported study. Wang's environment-art account describes player-summoned gusts expressed through foliage, cloth and particles. The production lesson is to reuse a recognizable environmental vocabulary for a player-requested cue, while retaining its atmospheric role.

Proposed review template. Start with one reachable destination and define ambient behavior, the player's request and intended directional meaning. Check whether visible carriers agree after a target change and remain legible in sparse and dense environments. Observe noticing the cue, interpreting direction and finding a traversable route separately. Retain the view, target, request time and route; ask what unfamiliar players believed the cue meant. This template has not been run.

The articles were originally published before the original game's launch, but display later dates too: May 29/June 10 and July 9/August 6, 2020. Passage-level revisions are unknown. They establish neither measured comprehension or accessibility gains nor pathfinding/obstacle-handling logic. Collectible assistance does not establish coverage of every item; a direction cue does not by itself identify a traversable route.

Cyberpunk 2077: Phantom Liberty: blockouts preserve constraints, not frozen layouts

Primary evidence and scope. CD PROJEKT RED’s October 2024 designer accounts for No Easy Way Out and The Killing Moon include development/final imagery and specific authoring decisions. These are retrospective examples, not current editable project files.

Published account. Andrzej Giełzak describes reducing a main-quest location to a smaller minor-quest scope while reusing its district footprint. A central statue was intended to prevent disorientation during spiral ascent. Combat retreat, sequential camera views and jump routes supported that progression; the ripperdoc room remained relatively low-detail pending cinematic requirements, with its gallery connection retained. Separately, Sebastian McBride recalls roughly five substantially different Maglev layouts, prioritizing circulation around a quest-critical booth. Mariusz Kubów describes changing the Viewing Gallery’s fundamental layout late in production and adding a prototyped hanging rocket.

Workflow template. Store the intended experience beside the blockout: scope, recognizable landmarks, playstyle routes, cinematic dependencies and the questions the draft is meant to answer. After a layout revision, compare matched views and traversal traces for each supported playstyle. Keep provisional spaces visibly provisional. Distinguish approval of narrative/spatial intent from approval of lighting, decoration, collision or final runtime behavior.

Limits. The published screenshots are curated artifacts. They do not expose all intermediate variants, source scenes, playtest data or exact game-build revisions. The reports show iteration and scope adaptation, without proving that a particular layout, tool or review method caused the shipped result’s quality.

Marvel’s Spider-Man: engine validation and crowd budgets

Primary source and scope. Noah Alzayer, Insomniac character TD, GDC March 2019 presentation, about the historical game pipeline.

Published production account. External facial-rig deliveries were clean files, while Insomniac preserved its additions separately. A tool converted pose/scan feedback into in-engine comparison videos. Relative skin loading preserved face weights while replacing body weights. Triangulation produced deformation artifacts absent in Maya; custom triangulation improved the silhouette. For open-world civilians and police, even simplified separate facial rigs proved too expensive. Combining heads with body rigs removed per-head joint positioning; most facial joints therefore translated rather than rotated. Generic clavicle weighting also caused clipping. See PDF pages 21–26, 34–49 and 52–64.

Workflow template. Evaluate exported characters at intended population density. Preserve aligned DCC/runtime views, rig/topology revisions, override provenance and frame/memory measurements. Test whether a second supplier delivery preserves local corrections. Judge silhouette, expression, clipping and crowd cost separately. This requires a project-specific export/capture path.

Ghost of Tsushima: lighting approximation costs

Primary source and scope. Jasmin Patry, Sucker Punch, Real-Time Samurai Cinema, SIGGRAPH 2021; author-hosted PDF with speaker notes. Historical PS4-era production.

Published production account. Dynamic sky lighting reused baked visibility data. A full transfer matrix was rejected for memory and capture cost. Thin-walled interiors exposed light leaks; cone occlusion, planes and low-resolution shadow maps remained unsatisfactory and expensive. The workable compromise classified probes as interior/exterior and used authored surface masks to weight interpolation. The notes explicitly balance runtime overhead against authoring burden. Remaining limits include static bake data, usually excluding movable/destructible objects, inaccurate bounce estimates, and no indirect illumination from local lights. See PDF pages 24–25, 51–60.

Test template. Test thin walls, moving objects, local lights and changing skies. Retain mask/probe revisions, before/after views and hardware timings. Separate unsupported contributions from regressions; count manual tagging alongside GPU cost. Keep art-directed acceptance explicit rather than automatically selecting the cheapest approximation.

Uncharted: Drake’s Fortune: early playable iteration

Primary source and scope. Neil Druckmann’s first-hand Uncharted postmortem, published 2008-10-08, concerns Naughty Dog’s original 2007 PS3 game. It is a retrospective, not independent evaluation.

Published production account. Ambitious general animation/AI systems became too complex; the team switched to focused solutions using layered full-body, partial and additive animation, with inverse kinematics for environmental registration. Separately, an overcomplicated tools effort delayed a pipeline for actually playing levels. Planned interchangeable enemy parts also held up enemy implementation before being abandoned. Subtle enemy designs were difficult to distinguish at gameplay distance, despite their plausibility close up. See the animation/AI discussion and numbered production-failure sections 2–4.

Workflow template. Require an early playable baseline before expanding authoring scope. Compare character variants from gameplay cameras and close-ups. Record movement/contact behavior and responsiveness alongside presentation. Track implementation delay, rework and iteration time separately from feature count. This does not establish that fewer features universally improve games.

Rainbow Six Siege: map handoffs before optimizing production

Primary source and scope. Ubisoft Montréal's studio account credits a Ubisoft Developers Conference 2020 presentation by Gaëlle Robert, Laura Metzlaff and Salomé Jugan. The article's exact publication date is unverified.

Published observations. The team mapped operator and elite-skin workflows, including task transfers and ideal versus actual time. Initial mapping took six months. Unprepared managers overestimated task duration; separate managers lacked end-to-end visibility and duplicated work. Elite-skin improvements were reinvested in quality within the existing three-month schedule. For operators, the team selected two similar characters for comparison across a nine-month cycle and reported one delivered with 50% fewer bugs without shortening production. The article does not provide absolute defect counts, severity distribution or a controlled comparison.

Workflow template. Evaluate an automation proposal against the whole delivery chain. Record active work, queueing, handoff, rework and acceptance time separately, together with the people and artifacts needed at each transition. Prepare participants before collecting estimates and compare sufficiently similar deliverables. Define whether the goal is shorter lead time, more creative iteration or fewer escaped defects before choosing a metric. Preserve the quality/time tradeoff rather than automatically translating saved task time into faster shipping. A proposed agent acceptance packet should identify the blocked dependency and responsible handoff, not merely report that its own transformation completed.

Boundary. This account supports a workflow-analysis lesson. It does not establish an accessible Ubisoft production interface or a generalizable defect-reduction effect.

EA SUPERNOVA: test selection has its own cost and error model

Primary source and scope. EA-authored SUPERNOVA, ICST 2022; inspected revision 2023-06-28. The paper describes historical internal SaaS; no public integration was verified.

Published observations. Risk-based selection ranked manual and automated tests using probability, impact and time factors with project-tuned weights. Separate commit prediction used gradient boosting and SZZ-derived labels, whose noise the authors discuss. One historical cohort reported 55% fewer testing hours. A different year-over-year planning comparison included 96 onboarding hours and 12 maintenance hours; the authors estimated approximately 75% first-year test-planning savings. Commit-risk results reported 71% precision/77% recall as macro averages, but only 49%/66% for the risky class, with 214 positive versus 1,215 negative test commits. Test generation and learned test selection remained future work.

Workflow template. Keep selection, execution and acceptance separate. Store the candidate-test population, selected and omitted IDs, risk criteria/weights, history window and human overrides. Evaluate time saved alongside missed regressions, defect severity, false alarms and onboarding/maintenance costs. Retain a protected audit sample outside the prioritizer's choices so that reduced observation is not mistaken for reduced risk. Report class-specific performance and label provenance before using a risk score to block changes; inspect a sample of both flagged and unflagged changes manually. No source result here validates replacing visual or gameplay judgment with that score.

Boundary. Observational production comparisons do not isolate causality or guarantee unchanged coverage. Their estimates are not a benchmark for an agent harness or evidence of current external availability.

Remedy Northlight: an edit can succeed without changing the composed world

Primary evidence and historical scope. Vesa Paakkanen's Building Game Worlds with OpenUSD and Sebastian Eriksson's Real-Time World Editing Technology in Northlight are first-hand GDC presentations dated 2024-03-20. Remedy explicitly released the presentations publicly on 2024-08-27. Despite that release page's Alan Wake 2 framing, Paakkanen's slide 19 identifies this USD scene pipeline's production users as Control 2 and Max Payne 1 & 2 Remake. It does not establish that Alan Wake 2 shipped with the new world editor.

Published production account. The old editor's manual-export workflow and inconsistent reuse concepts motivated a replacement. Remedy wrapped references, variants and sublayers into reusable “bundles,” building their layer stacks dynamically to avoid shared Perforce-file conflicts and permit personal layers. Composition introduced another failure surface: a property could be written successfully but remain hidden by a stronger opinion. Renaming nodes could break overrides and relationships. The proposed response included context visualization, stronger-opinion indicators and substantial supporting tooling; reuse did not remove maintenance complexity. See Paakkanen slides 8, 52, 63–64 and 73–82.

Eriksson separates authoring, disk and runtime representations, explicitly focusing on entities/components rather than asset conversion. Export-time scene access records dependencies. Opening a scene or exporting on builders performs a complete in-memory export instead of persisting a cache and dependency graph; the team reported roughly seconds for its production-level export and accepted that work to avoid stale disk data. Subsequent edits re-export affected entities and rebuild their ECS components in the running game. See slides 11, 25–38, 44–53. This is a deliberate freshness/complexity tradeoff, not a universal argument against caches.

Acceptance template. An editing acceptance record should preserve the requested target layer, authored opinion, strongest composed value, affected dependencies and observed runtime result. A proposed fixture would edit a weaker variant, rename a referenced node and change a dependency outside the visible selection, then compare live-edit results with a clean reopen/export. Treat “write accepted,” “visible change” and “reference integrity preserved” as separate outcomes. Record source-schema and exporter versions independently of runtime layout. Previewing another contributor's layer should include provenance and the intended composition order, with review before destructive cleanup. Compare camera turns, forward attacks and lateral movement in an open arena and a constrained passage, including insufficient-space fallbacks. Preserve enemy mix, resources and difficulty between revisions. Record the player view beside enemy positions, aggression/attack transitions, indicator changes and received damage. Ask unfamiliar players afterward where they expected unseen enemies to be and what each warning meant; keep their answers separate from the designer's interpretation. Check whether a positional revision improves prediction while preserving meaningful defensive decisions. Repeat with supported camera settings and cue alternatives. This project-specific template has not been run; the presentation does not supply its fixture or acceptance thresholds.

Horizon Forbidden West: carry player opportunities from creature brief to in-game asset

Choose the opportunity a creature should offer before refining its surface detail: observe a state change, approach an exposed part or choose a different attack. Keep that opportunity visible through concept, animation and replacement-asset reviews.

Published method. Guerrilla's November 8, 2021 article quotes Principal Machine Designer Blake Politeski and Asset Art Lead Maxim Fleury about the then-upcoming game. A brief specifies appearance, interactive parts and attacks. A 3D concept receives art-direction, design, animation and technical-art review. A rigged, animated model checks functionality before detailed modeling; further review precedes replacing the temporary in-game model. Fleury describes lengthy iteration and the challenge of adding functionality while retaining the established design language.

Sunwing's solar-harvesting identity creates a vulnerable harvesting state versus greater idle alertness. The article describes textures indicating interactive parts and sounds distinguishing idling, alerting others and initiating attacks. These are related design intentions, not evidence that audio participated in the enumerated concept-review sequence. The pre-release account supplies no measured comprehension, production savings or numerical exposure windows. Primary account

Proposed workflow template. Use an original project-owned enemy and record the player decision, relevant states, available actions and observable consequences. Annotate a rough component layout with gameplay functions, then review it in motion from the player camera, including poses that conceal or overlap parts. Have art, design and animation review the same intended opportunity; retain unresolved problems and the revision that changes its exposure or meaning.

After replacing the temporary asset, repeat the same state sequence and approach conditions. Compare the intended opportunity with actual component, motion and sound behavior. Ask unfamiliar players which action they believed was available and why; retain their actions separately from those explanations. Distinguish an unavailable action, missed cue and misunderstood consequence before revising the asset. This template is unrun. The article does not disclose component-placement rules, acceptance thresholds or audio-authoring handoffs; those need project-specific decisions.

Hitman: expose a viable approach without requiring its guide

Keep the mission's possibilities separate from optional guidance. A player should be able to discover and pursue an approach without first activating its information sequence.

Published method. Jacob Mikkelsen and Mette Pødenphant Andersen's GDC Europe 2016 account covers the 2016 game's prologue and Paris guidance design. The primary deck reports closed-alpha navigation/overview confusion and perceived emptiness. For a later challenge-handout prototype, the authors report self-chosen goals, experimentation and investigation of NPC reactions. PDF pp.55–59

The authoring contract requires information not to gate progression, the opportunity to function without its guide, and guidance to end before the kill. PDF p.105 In the model-disguise example, the opportunity is shown as completed on reaching a private audience, before the target is eliminated. PDF pp.90–91

The shown disposal-before-disguise and discovery/gunfire sequences end in “Opportunity Failed” and “Opportunity Missed”, respectively; the latter also shows target lockdown. These examples do not establish recovery after arbitrary abandonment, guide switching or re-entry. PDF pp.93–97

Proposed review template. Choose a mission with two materially different viable approaches. Record the end goal, actor/item prerequisites, access rules, information cues and guide states separately. Define which changes remove an approach and which affect only player knowledge. Try starting without the hint, discovering it after progress, disabling guidance, removing a prerequisite, causing an alert, switching plans and attempting re-entry. Compare the displayed guide status with remaining world possibilities.

Retain the mission revision, initial state, event order, guide settings and visible messages. Ask unfamiliar players what they intend to try and why; keep those explanations separate from observed actions. Report state consistency, discoverability and player initiative independently. This template is unrun. The deck's prototype observations are qualitative and give no participant count or controlled comparison; its closing replay-success illustration is a press headline, not retention data. The account does not establish current World of Assassination behavior. PDF p.111

VALORANT: separate predicted views from authoritative results and visible feedback

Historical simulation account. Matt deWet and David Straily's July 2020 netcode article describes server authority with local prediction. Buffering smooths uneven movement delivery while delaying observation. Fixed-rate movement/physics updates are separate from rendering; missing moves can trigger server prediction and subsequent client correction. Their blind peeking experiments varied server tick rate, latency, client frame rate and weapons. These developer-reported experiments are not a public benchmark or universal threshold. Ping alone does not describe queueing, simulation and presentation delay.

Correctness and clarity differ. Kevin Lee's August 2020 hit-registration account distinguishes wrong shot resolution from misleading feedback. Immediate tracer/muzzle feedback appears after firing input; hit confirmation waits for the server. Rewound target positions/poses can differ from those visible when feedback arrives. Moving/crouching targets and delayed impact effects can create misleading overlap. Attaching effects to body parts was an experiment with its own cover-related ambiguity, not a shipped guarantee. The account acknowledges correctness bugs too; a confusing video is neither sufficient proof of a simulation fault nor grounds to dismiss one.

Tuning is version-specific. Patch 6.02, February 2023 reduced maximum rewind from 200 ms to 140 ms. Riot expected fewer apparent deaths behind cover against high-ping opponents, with possible hit-registration inaccuracy for those opponents. This is a historical fairness tradeoff, not evidence of today's configuration.

Proposed evidence fixture. In a project-owned scenario, retain build, movement/weapon, network impairment, simulation/render rates and timeline uncertainty. Link input, predicted state, server evaluation, correction, result receipt and visible feedback frames. Judge authoritative invariants and feedback clarity separately; incomplete traces remain inconclusive. This unrun fixture complements transport/application checks; it does not establish reconnect persistence or long-duration soak reliability.

Legends of Runeterra: separate iteration success from merge readiness

Historical production workflow. Guy Kisel's May 2021 account distinguishes single-platform Iteration builds, which omitted extra validation, from full Merge Readiness builds required before merging. A successful Iteration run still set a failing commit status to discourage treating its narrower success as readiness.

Branch-specific test environments replaced shared sandboxes where developers could interfere with one another. Logical isolation did not require dedicated servers: servers hosted data for multiple environments. Each branch had its own pipeline. A JSON build plan recorded artifacts, hashes and dirty state to decide what needed rebuilding.

Regex-based failure analysis produced ambiguous false positives. The team moved failure definitions into build scripts, emitting structured JSON at the failure site for telemetry and notifications. These documented practices do not establish present access to Riot's internal systems or general performance gains.

Proposed evidence fixture. Record build mode, revision, platform/game-version scope, omitted validations, logical environment and shared-server identity, artifact hashes and failure provenance. Iteration success must not satisfy an unrun merge gate. Compare source-emitted failure records with raw evidence instead of trusting either a keyword match or a structured label alone. Reuse the build/artifact workflow, with missing required results kept unknown. This synthetic contract is unimplemented; it does not prescribe Riot's branching strategy for GameMap.

Rainbow Six Siege: author destruction as a gameplay surface with material rules

Make an opening useful and legible without requiring every visible fragment to become an authoritative physical object. Destruction changes the authored environment, so its visual, collision and gameplay representations need deliberate boundaries.

Historical authoring method. Julien L’Heureux's GDC 2016 deck describes destruction-ready modeling and artist training. Assets separate parts by physical material. Planar-surface cuts combine impact position, cutter inputs and material parameters; artists can author tiled texture-cutter motifs in UV space. Geometry decorations survive cutting into child surfaces at added CPU, rendering, memory and authoring cost; feature-bound decorations instead disappear when their supporting feature disappears. PDF pp. 29–47

Gameplay needs an explicit interpretation of when an object is broken, alongside navigation, visibility and acoustic updates. Simplified collision removes small holes and reduces hole tessellation, while shooting can use detailed surface geometry. Dynamic debris uses replacements and aggressive recycling rather than procedurally cut moving fragments. Asynchronous work can delay perception relative to destruction state; the deck describes pre-destruction synchronized to an animation's end. These are historical production choices, not universal physics requirements or present-day engine contracts. PDF pp. 24–27, 52–55, 62–64

Damage is a separate authored consequence. Ubisoft's February 2020 Y5S1 announcement describes explosion-shape queries, entity-specific raycast points and damage-blocking metadata. Damage and destruction can have different shapes and ranges. The proposed shrapnel change would reduce damage according to the number of intervening destructible objects rather than let them cap its range; holes were intended to communicate damage direction. The article's realism and clarity claims are design goals, not measured improvements or verification of current behavior.

Proposed workflow template. Author one original wall assembly with material boundaries, decorative features, intended openings and explicit break-state rules. Preserve its source revision and cutter parameters. Compare repeated impacts and an explosion, recording visible geometry, movement collision, shooting queries, navigation/acoustic readiness and gameplay events separately. Ask whether players can infer which opening permits sight, traversal or damage before interpreting debris as evidence.

Retain the event timeline and distinguish accepted work, geometry completion and dependent-system readiness. Review intended exceptions rather than requiring exact visual/collision equality. Compare authored character with cost and complexity at the same project settings. This unrun template complements navigation readiness; neither source supplies a public RealBlast adapter, reusable game assets or a project-independent acceptance threshold.
